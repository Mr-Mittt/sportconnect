# Background jobs and runners

Every scheduled job, start-up runner and background worker the Java server runs, in one place (the counterpart of `REDIS_KEYS.md` and `infra/documentation/PORTS.md`). **Add the row in the same change that adds a job.** Written 2026-11-23 (SESSION-53 pickup) from the code: the intervals and switches below were read from the classes named in the table, not from memory.

**One scheduler thread.** `SchedulingConfig` only enables `@Scheduled`; no pool size is set, so Spring's default applies: all `@Scheduled` methods of the server share **one thread**. A slow job delays every other tick. That is why a job that can run long does not do its work on the scheduler thread: it only *nudges* a dedicated worker thread (the group-session generation worker, the statistics worker).

## When it fails, and what retries it

| Job | A failure does | What retries it, and when | A cap, and what the operator sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outbox relays (session, user) | A publish that is nacked or times out (5 s confirm) leaves the row `PENDING`, bumps `attemptCount` and `lastAttemptAt`, logs a warning, never throws | The next tick, every 10 s, takes it again (the fetch takes pending rows oldest first) | **No cap**: a row that never publishes is retried for ever, so it stays at the head of the queue; `attemptCount` and the warning in the log are the signal. Filed as common **C14** (backoff, a cap, no head-of-line blocking) |
| Session lifecycle | Each of the three methods is one `@Transactional`: a failure rolls that method back | The next 15-minute cron run does the same work again (nothing is queued, the query finds the sessions still due) | No cap; the scheduler logs the exception |
| Group session generation | One slot per `REQUIRES_NEW` transaction; a failed slot is counted under its row lock | Backoff 1, 5, 15, 60 minutes, read by the 60-second tick; `FAILED` on the fifth attempt, revived by the next planning (an edit, a session ending, or start-up) | Capped at five attempts; `FAILED` slots and their last error are on the queue row |
| Venue backfill | A failure of the whole run is logged at error and never stops start-up; a venue that resolves to nothing stays unlinked | The next start of the server, which tries every still-unlinked venue once | No cap; the log line `examined / linked / left unresolved` |
| Statistics engine (SESSION-53) | The session's counting rolls back as a whole; the failure is recorded in a separate small transaction (`RETRY`, `next_attempt_at` 15 min ahead, `last_error`) | The 5-minute tick takes a `RETRY` row once it is due (the third tick after the failure); a `RUNNING` claim older than 10 minutes is taken over; `FAILED` is taken again after 6 hours; a claim on a sport switched off meanwhile is deleted, so the session is counted once the sport is switched on | `FAILED` after four attempts; `attempts`, `last_error` and `result` on the job row |
| Leaderboard ranking (SESSION-54) | A board is claimed (compare-and-set on `stat_board_runs`), built from reads and published in ONE transaction; a failure rolls that board back and is recorded in a separate transaction (`status FAILED`, `last_error`, `next_attempt_at` = now + `retry-minutes`, 30); it never stops the other boards, and the change rows are untouched | The retry tick takes a `FAILED` board once it is due and the nightly run takes any dirty board whose time has passed; a `RUNNING` claim older than 15 minutes is taken over (a publication from the old claim rolls back); a country built while one of its region boards still had another sort list stays dirty and is rebuilt in the next pass | No cap on attempts (a board that keeps failing is tried every 30 minutes); `stat_board_runs.status` and `last_error` and the log line per board are the signal; a pass takes at most `max-boards` (500) boards and 45 minutes |
| Group season status (SESSION-55) | Each season is advanced in its own `REQUIRES_NEW` transaction (`SeasonTransitionStep`); a failure rolls that season back and leaves every other alone | The next 5-minute tick reads the season again from its stored status (nothing is queued; the clock decides what is due, so a job that was down for days takes both steps at once). The season row is taken with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` (the lock an owner's edit, cancel and link change also take): a season someone holds is skipped and done by the next tick, and the status is re-read under the lock, so a season cancelled a moment ago is never started | No cap (a season that keeps failing is tried every tick). The operator sees an error log line per season and its `group_seasons.last_error` (cleared by the next success) |

## Two things every job here must answer (design review)

- **Two at once.** Name the guard: a unique key (the loser fails, so absorb the error), a row lock such as `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` (the loser skips), or a compare-and-set update. Every transaction has its own connection, so threads of one instance race like two instances.
- **What the clock is.** A backoff with nothing to wake it is not a retry; say which tick reads `next_attempt_at`.

## Season counting (SESSION-55 slice 2)
Not a job of its own: `SessionStatsProcessor.process` calls `SeasonStatsCounter.countSession` in its own counting transaction, so every completed group session the engine counts is also counted into the `ONGOING` or `COMPLETED` seasons that want it (a failure rolls the whole session back and the engine's `RETRY` clock retries it). The seasons' own safety net for sessions the engine has not counted is the finalise sweep above. Row locks: the season row first (`FOR UPDATE`, refreshed after the lock), then its member rows in user-id order, then its head-to-head row.
